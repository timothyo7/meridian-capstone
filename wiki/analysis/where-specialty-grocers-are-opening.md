# Where specialty grocers are opening

A filed answer to the question: *"What do our sources say about where
specialty grocers are opening?"* Answered on 2026-09-22 from the four sources
ingested at that point. Every claim cites a source page. Conclusions the
sources don't state are labeled `*Team inference:*`.

**Short answer:** the sources name only a few specific places. Most of the
evidence is about *how* specialty grocers are opening rather than *where*.

## Specific locations named

**Pasadena area: Sprouts**
([Colorado Boulevard, Mar 2026](../sources/sprouts-pasadena-2026.md))

- [Sprouts](../entities/sprouts.md) already has a store in **unincorporated
  East Pasadena**, at Rosemead and Colorado Boulevards.
- As of March 11, 2026, Sprouts was named as the applicant on a
  change-of-ownership notice at a **former Rite Aid, 1421 E. Washington
  Blvd.** "If approved," it would be the first Sprouts inside Pasadena city
  limits. The article gives no opening date and doesn't say what the
  application is for.
- Sprouts has **signed a lease on a former 99 Cents Only Store at 6235 York
  Blvd., Highland Park**, a few miles away, with opening expected in 2027.

**Orange County: Gelson's**
([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md))

- [Gelson's](../entities/gelsons.md), which runs 27 traditional stores in
  Southern California, has opened two small "premium convenience"
  micromarkets paired with EV charging: **3,000 sq ft in Santa Ana** and
  **5,400 sq ft in Costa Mesa**.
- *Team inference:* this rests on the cities named above. Santa Ana and Costa
  Mesa are in Orange County, one of Meridian's three counties
  ([brief](../sources/client-brief.md)). The article itself calls Costa Mesa
  part of "the Los Angeles area," and the county is the team's general
  knowledge.

**Inland Empire: Whole Foods**
([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md))

- Regency Centers is developing a Whole Foods-anchored center in **Redlands,
  CA**, with a 36,000 sq ft grocery store. The article presents this as a real
  estate development, not as specialty-grocer expansion.

## How they're opening (patterns, not places)

- **In buildings other chains left behind.** Both of Sprouts' planned stores
  are in closed Rite Aid and 99 Cents Only buildings
  ([Colorado Boulevard, Mar 2026](../sources/sprouts-pasadena-2026.md)).
- **In smaller formats.** FMI reports that U.S. grocery stores are shrinking,
  and Gelson's micromarkets take that to 3,000–5,400 sq ft
  ([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)).
- **Where landlords want them as anchors.** Grocery-anchored centers have
  vacancy "well below the broader shopping center market" and command rent
  premiums. However, "the days when any grocery store could anchor a center
  equally well are fading"
  ([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)).
- **At a fast pace.** Sprouts plans "at least 40 new stores in 2026"
  ([Colorado Boulevard, Mar 2026](../sources/sprouts-pasadena-2026.md)).
  Specialty grocers are gaining market share, with Trader Joe's leading the
  segment, but the source gives no store locations for Trader Joe's
  ([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)).
- The McKinsey source says nothing about where stores open. Its relevant point
  is that "regional grocers still do have a right to win" on "local relevance"
  ([Shelby/McKinsey, Sep 2026](../sources/shelby-mckinsey-grocery-2026.md)).

## What the sources don't tell us

- Nothing about Ventura County, or about LA County outside the Pasadena and
  Highland Park corridor.
- No count or map of specialty grocery openings in Meridian's three counties,
  so Q22 in the [register](open-questions.md) is still open.
- Aldi's 150 new stores and the Walmart and Costco openings in the ICSC article
  are discount and big-box chains, not specialty grocers, so they are left out
  here ([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)).
- A web search on 2026-09-22 turned up Trader Joe's plans for Anaheim Hills and
  a Grocery Outlet in South Pasadena. Neither has been ingested, so neither is
  cited here.

## Team conclusions (not stated by any source)

1. *Team inference:* this rests on the Sprouts points above. The Pasadena area
   is getting attention from specialty grocers. Sprouts has one store there
   and wants a second inside the city. That's evidence of demand, and also of
   competition, for Meridian's "obvious next step"
   ([Pasadena site](../entities/pasadena-site.md)).
2. *Team inference:* this rests on the Sprouts sites and on Meridian's growth
   by taking over vacated leases ([brief](../sources/client-brief.md)).
   Meridian's growth playbook is no longer unusual. Sprouts is doing the same
   thing locally, which could make that kind of space scarcer or more
   expensive (Q25; see [Expansion strategy](../concepts/expansion-strategy.md)).
3. *Team inference:* this rests on the gaps listed above. The evidence is
   thin: one competitor in one corridor, plus a small-format trend in Orange
   County. It's enough to raise Q28 with Dana, but not enough to draw
   conclusions about the market.
