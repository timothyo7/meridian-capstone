# Differentiation

What Meridian says sets it apart from national grocery chains, per the
[client brief](../sources/client-brief.md).

## What we know

- Meridian competes on prepared foods, local sourcing, and a smaller footprint
  than national chains ([brief](../sources/client-brief.md)).
- The brief offers no evidence about whether these actually drive performance
  ([brief](../sources/client-brief.md)).
- The category dimension of the requested dashboard is where this gets tested
  — see [Engagement scope](engagement-scope.md).

Sector evidence on each claimed advantage:

- **Positioning:** specialty grocers are gaining share, and traditional
  supermarkets are "caught in the middle" between hard discounters and curated
  specialty stores ([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)).
- **Prepared foods:** grocery is taking dining occasions from fast-food and
  fast-casual chains, based on self-reported survey data. Target is also
  "investing meaningfully in the in-store food experience" ([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)).
- **Smaller footprint:** stores are getting smaller across the industry. FMI
  reports shrinking U.S. stores, Gelson's has opened 3,000–5,400 sq ft
  micromarkets in Southern California, and Aldi is piloting a modular layout
  for smaller urban footprints ([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)).
- **Local sourcing:** the source does not address it ([ICSC, Apr 2026](../sources/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)).
- *Team inference:* this rests on the points above. Meridian's
  differentiators follow the sector's growth trends, so they may be getting
  less distinctive as larger chains and [Gelson's](../entities/gelsons.md)
  move the same way. The source says nothing about Meridian itself.
