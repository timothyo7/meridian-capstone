# New grocery formats, store growth and investment — ICSC (John Egan), April 24, 2026

## What it says

A trade-press roundup from ICSC's *Commerce + Communities Today*, written for
shopping-center owners and investors. It reports on the grocery sector in
eight short sections: national chains reworking their formats and identities,
specialty grocers gaining share, big-box chains adding stores, investors buying
grocery-anchored centers, grocery taking dining occasions from restaurants, and
small-format stores in Southern California. Most figures come from other
sources (Consumer Edge, JLL, IBISWorld, Tillster, FMI, Forbes, The Shelby
Report). ICSC is repeating them, not publishing its own research. Nothing in the
article is about Meridian.

## Key claims

Sector and share:
- IBISWorld estimates U.S. grocery as a $912.4 billion sector.
- Traditional grocers still get most shopping visits, but their market share
  is slipping. Value-oriented and fresh-format concepts are driving growth
  (JLL's Naveen Jaggi, via a LinkedIn article).
- "Specialty grocers are picking up market share." Trader Joe's grew 3% year
  over year for the period ending Feb. 28 and led the specialty sector. It
  outperformed the whole grocery sector by 6 percentage points (Consumer Edge,
  *U.S. Grocery Outlook 2026*). **The article does not say what the 3%
  measures** (sales, transactions, or visits), and it does not say which year
  the period ending Feb. 28 belongs to.
- Traditional grocers such as Publix and Safeway "are seeing pullbacks from
  every income group." Discount grocery growth has "plateaued since mid-2025"
  (Consumer Edge).
- Shoppers are "gravitating toward retailers that give them a clear reason to
  be loyal" (value, or a curated specialty experience), and "traditional
  supermarkets are caught in the middle" (Consumer Edge's Michael Gunther).

Real estate:
- Grocery-anchored centers have vacancy "well below the broader shopping
  center market" and "continue to command rent premiums." However, "the days
  when any grocery store could anchor a center equally well are fading"
  (Jaggi).
- Deals for U.S. grocery-anchored centers totaled almost $11 billion in 2025,
  up 42% from 2024 (JLL *Grocery Tracker 2026*). This is a dollar volume of
  transactions, not a count of stores.
- Brixton Capital bought the Vallarta Supermarkets-anchored Quad at Whittier
  in suburban Los Angeles for $100 million. It was 95.6% leased at purchase.
- Retail construction "has slowed considerably," but some developers are
  still building grocery-anchored centers. One example is a Whole Foods-anchored
  center in Redlands, CA, with a 36,000 sq ft grocery store.

Formats:
- FMI says U.S. grocery stores are getting smaller.
- Gelson's (27 traditional stores in Southern California) has opened two
  "premium convenience" micromarkets paired with EV charging: 3,000 sq ft in
  Santa Ana and 5,400 sq ft in Costa Mesa (via The Shelby Report).
- Aldi is piloting a modular layout that "can be adapted across store sizes
  and formats … to smaller urban footprints" (via Forbes).
- Target is repositioning its grocery business "Trader Joe's-style," around
  owned brands, wellness, and the in-store food experience.

Prepared food and dining:
- Tillster's *2026 Phygital Index* surveyed 2,144 U.S. diners. 36% said they
  visit grocery stores more often, and 29% and 37% said they visit fast-food
  and fast-casual chains less often. These are **self-reported changes in
  frequency, not sales**. The article also notes that Tillster sells technology
  to restaurant brands.

## Questions it raises

- Meridian says it competes on prepared foods, local sourcing, and a smaller
  footprint. This article describes national and regional players moving
  toward all three. Who does Meridian actually compete against? That is Q24
  in the [register](../analysis/open-questions.md).
- Meridian grew mostly by taking over leases vacated by other chains. This
  article describes a grocery-anchored real estate market with low vacancy and
  rent premiums. Whether that route is still open is Q25.
- Q22 (the specialty competitors in Meridian's three counties) is only
  partly informed. Gelson's is one named Southern California operator, but the
  article gives no county-level map of competitors.
- The article is national and mostly about large chains. It has no data on
  regional specialty chains the size of Meridian (14 stores, about $78M).
