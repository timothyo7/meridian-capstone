# McKinsey's seven forces shaping 2026 grocery — The Shelby Report (Treva Bennett), September 14, 2026

## What it says

A trade-press account of McKinsey & Co.'s *The State of Grocery North
America 2026*, based on a webinar in which four McKinsey retail partners
presented it. The report was released in June 2026. It draws on a March
survey of nearly 5,000 grocery shoppers and an April survey of more than 40
grocery executives in the U.S. and Canada. It groups the industry around
seven forces: value, private brands, fresh, wellness, e-commerce, retail
media, and AI. **This is a secondhand account.** Claude could not retrieve the McKinsey
page (it timed out), so the figures here are McKinsey's as the Shelby Report
tells it. **On 2026-09-22 the team checked the headline figures against the
McKinsey web page and they matched:** sales up 1.2%, prices up 2.2%, and
volumes down 1% in 2025. Nobody has checked the other figures against the
original. Nothing in the article is about Meridian.

## Key claims

Market:
- U.S. grocery sales grew 1.2% in 2025. Prices rose 2.2% and volumes fell
  1%: "a market that grew in dollars as shoppers bought fewer units."
  Verified by the team against the McKinsey web page on 2026-09-22.
- Units per trip fell in every channel except drug over the 12 months ended
  June 2026, and how often shoppers buy has started to decline.
- More than half of shoppers say they are cutting impulse purchases. About
  80% of grocers plan to prioritize key value items in pricing.
- "Regional grocers still do have a right to win. The biggest assets that
  regional grocers have are the local relevance, trusted shopper relationships
  and priority audiences" (McKinsey's Joshua Reuben).

Fresh and prepared foods:
- 43% of consumers visit a retailer for its fresh offering. 42% are more
  likely to buy additional packaged items when buying fresh or prepared foods.
- Prepared foods purchase frequency is up 9% year over year, and "about a
  quarter of consumers" replace restaurant orders with grocery-prepared food.
  The article does not give the period or the population for either figure.
- Grocers name cost pressures (88%), labor availability and skill (78%), and
  supply chain complexity (69%) as barriers to scaling fresh. These are shares
  of the roughly 40 surveyed executives.
- Grocers are told to choose "where do you want to be famous? What's your hero
  product?" and to define what they stand for: fresh, prepared foods, wellness,
  or private label.

Loyalty and private label:
- Grocers expect personalized promotions to rise from 35% to 55% of promotions
  within two to three years, "with loyalty programs as the engine."
- "Loyalty members who redeem personalized offers spend 4.3 times more each
  year than those who do not." **This is a comparison between groups, not a
  measured effect.** Customers who redeem offers may simply be heavier shoppers
  to begin with.
- Private label grew about three times faster than national brands in 2025,
  reaching 20.7% of North American packaged food and beverage retail sales.
  85% of consumers say private label matches or beats national brands in
  quality.

Wellness:
- 16% of consumers report current household use of GLP-1 medications. Those
  households report buying smaller portions and fewer snacks, and shifting
  spend toward produce and protein.

Also covered but less relevant to this engagement: delivery vs. pickup,
e-commerce profitability, retail media economics, and AI shopping agents.

## Questions it raises

- The brief lists prepared foods and local sourcing as Meridian's
  differentiators but says nothing about owned brands or private label, which
  this source calls a major differentiator. Does Meridian have one? That is
  Q26 in the [register](../analysis/open-questions.md).
- If industry dollar sales grew while volumes fell, Meridian's store-by-week
  sales totals may look like growth when they aren't. Separating price from
  volume is Q27.
- The industry names labor as a main barrier to fresh food. That links
  Meridian's prepared-foods advantage to Q17 (what drives cost differences
  between stores).
- The 4.3× loyalty figure strengthens the case for asking Q18 (has the
  loyalty program ever been used?). Any analysis of Meridian's own members
  stays Restricted and outside this wiki.
